A reflective model gives the piece a defensible structure. Gibbs' reflective
cycle (Gibbs, 1988) is the common choice on this programme; Rolfe's *What? So
what? Now what?* is a leaner alternative. Pick one, name it, and follow it —
the headings below use Gibbs and should be replaced with prose in the final
version.

## Description

<div class="todo" markdown="1">
What happened over the module — briefly. This section should be the shortest;
under a strict word limit, description is the cheapest thing to cut.
</div>

## Feelings

<div class="todo" markdown="1">
Your honest reaction, particularly to the team work and to any point where the
technical work stalled. Resilience is a named skill for this module, and it can
only be evidenced by describing something that was genuinely difficult.
</div>

## Evaluation

<div class="todo" markdown="1">
What went well and what did not. Reference specific artefacts.
</div>

## Analysis

<div class="todo" markdown="1">
Why it went that way. This is where the marks are: connect your experience to
the module's concepts — agent architectures, coordination, uncertainty — and to
the literature. Address all four learning outcomes here.
</div>

## Conclusion

<div class="todo" markdown="1">
What you now understand that you did not at the start, and what you would do
differently.
</div>

## Action plan

<div class="todo" markdown="1">
What you will carry into the next module. Keep this consistent with the
[PDP](../skills/) — the two should not tell different stories.
</div>
