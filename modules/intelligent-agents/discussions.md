## Discussion 1 — Agent-based systems in organisations

<p class="meta"><span class="muted">August 2026</span></p>

<div class="learned" markdown="1">
<p class="learned-title">What I learned</p>

**From my research**

- Reactive and BDI agents follow fixed plans and struggle with change. LLMs add
  reasoning, task decomposition and memory, at a cost in reliability and
  governance.

**From the discussion**

- **Reliability is an architecture problem.** Isaac showed that errors spread
  at handoffs, so verify there and test agents in a sandbox first.
- **Autonomy must be bounded.** Panagiotis argued that independence should
  match the cost of failure, and Imoh that humans keep accountability.
- **Control works best as a hybrid.** In Luis's thread, an LLM that eases use
  brings hallucination, so it needs guardrails and central governance.

</div>

## Discussion 3 — Ethics of generative AI

<p class="meta"><span class="muted">October 2026</span></p>

<div class="learned" markdown="1">
<p class="learned-title">What I learned</p>

**From my research**

- Generative AI's main risks are copyright, from training on scraped work, and
  misinformation, from realistic synthetic media. Both come from learning on
  vast, uncurated data.

**From the discussion**

- **I overstated my evidence.** Lara showed that deepfakes have been *used* in
  elections, not shown to *change* results. The defensible harm is lost trust
  in authentic information.
- **Oversight needs specific safeguards.** Isaac turned my "careful oversight"
  into checks at each stage: record data provenance before training, test
  outputs before deployment, and do not rely on labels alone at distribution.
- **Every safeguard has a trade-off.** My research showed watermarking trades
  invisibility, speed and robustness. Isaac added that it can confirm machine
  generation, never human authorship.

</div>
